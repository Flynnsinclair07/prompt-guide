Best ChatGPT Prompt for Doctor Appointment Prep

Walk in organized: symptom timeline, focused questions, and what to ask if you're not satisfied with the answer.

The Prompt 
You are a primary-care doctor who is honest about how rushed appointments are and how to make the most of 15 minutes. Help me prep for an upcoming appointment.

What kind of appointment: [primary care / specialist (which kind) / urgent care / follow-up]
What's bringing me in (in my own words, no jargon): [DESCRIBE]
How long this has been going on: [DAYS / WEEKS / MONTHS]
Symptoms (be specific, including what makes them better/worse):
  - [SYMPTOM 1 + when, severity 1-10, triggers]
  - [SYMPTOM 2]
What I've already tried (medications, home remedies, lifestyle changes): [LIST]
My relevant history (medications, conditions, family history if relevant): [BRIEF]
What I'm hoping to leave the appointment with: [diagnosis / test / referral / med / reassurance / plan]

Requirements:
- Give me a SHORT (under 2 minute) opening summary I can read aloud to the doctor — not a medical essay
- List 5-8 specific questions to ask, in order of importance
- For each question, tell me what a satisfactory answer looks like AND what to ask if the answer is vague
- Flag any symptoms in my list that warrant earlier care (ER, urgent care today)
- Do NOT diagnose me. Do NOT recommend specific medications. This is appointment prep, not medical advice.
- If something I described could be serious, say so plainly and tell me to call my doctor or 911 NOW — not wait for the appointment

How to Use This Prompt

  1. Write down your symptoms BEFORE you talk to the model — specific symptoms, timeline, severity 1-10
  2. Include what you've already tried — this saves 5 minutes in the appointment and shows the doctor you're prepared
  3. Be honest about what you want to leave with — if you want a referral, say so; the questions look different
  4. Paste into ChatGPT or Claude
  5. Read the “flag earlier care” section first — if anything in your symptoms warrants urgent care, don't wait for the appointment

This is appointment prep, not medical advice. ChatGPT does not have your chart, your vitals, or the ability to examine you. The point of this prompt is to walk in organized — not to skip the doctor.

Example Output

Task: Prep for a primary-care appointment about persistent fatigue

Response:

Opening summary (read aloud, ~90 seconds):
“I've been more tired than usual for about 3 months. It's not exhaustion after a busy day — it's tired even after a full night of sleep. I've tried going to bed earlier, cutting back on coffee after noon, and a multivitamin, no change. I'm sleeping 7-8 hours and tracked it with my watch. I'm a 32-year-old woman, periods are regular but heavier than 2 years ago. No new medications. My mom was hypothyroid — diagnosed at 35. I'd like to figure out what to test for.”

Questions to ask (in order):
  1. Given the symptoms and family history, what blood panels would you order today?
     [Satisfactory answer names specifics: CBC, ferritin, TSH, free T4, vitamin D, B12.
      If vague: ask “Can we run a full iron panel and a thyroid panel today?”]
  2. If everything comes back normal, what's the next step?
     [Satisfactory answer names a timeline and a referral path.]
  3. Could the heavier periods be related to the fatigue?
     [Satisfactory answer addresses ferritin specifically.]
  ... (5 more)

When to call earlier:
  If you develop shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, or fainting,
  go to the ER — don't wait for the appointment.

Tips to Get Better Results

  • Specifics beat severity words. “Tired even after 8 hours of sleep, started 3 months ago” gets a useful response. “Really tired” does not.
  • Bring the summary, on paper. Hand the doctor a printed half-page. Most will appreciate it; it's faster than them reconstructing your story.
  • Ask what tests, not what diagnosis. Doctors will run the tests; getting them to commit to a diagnosis in 15 minutes is unreasonable.
  • If the answer is vague, ask one follow-up. “What specifically would we be testing for?” is a fair question. Two follow-ups is also fair. After that, you're past appointment length.
  • This is appointment prep, not medical advice. ChatGPT can't see you. Anything that feels urgent today — chest pain, breathing trouble, severe pain — is a 911 call or urgent care, not a chatbot.
