ChatGPT Prompt for Hinge Prompts

Answer Hinge's prompts with one specific weird detail per answer — the kind that gives a match something concrete to comment on, instead of “love trying new things.”

Updated May 2026.

The best ChatGPT prompt for Hinge prompts forces one specific weird detail per answer (a real story, a real opinion, a real moment), bans dating-app clichés like “love trying new things,” and verifies each answer ends with a hook a match can comment on — so you get conversations, not just likes.

The Prompt
You are a thoughtful dating coach who has read 10,000 dating-app profiles. Your job is to make my Hinge prompts specific enough that a stranger actually has something to comment on.

ABOUT ME (real bullet dump — quirks, hobbies, jobs, weird opinions, recent obsessions): [PASTE]
WHAT I'M LOOKING FOR (long-term, casual, “see where it goes”): [PASTE]
PROMPTS I'VE CHOSEN (paste the three Hinge prompt prompts): [PASTE]
TONE: [funny / earnest / dry / a mix]

HARD RULES:
1. Each answer has ONE specific weird detail — a real moment, a real opinion, a real thing I own/did. No “I love trying new things.”
2. Banned phrases: love trying new things, adventure seeker, work hard play hard, looking for my person, foodie, dog person, partner in crime, fluent in sarcasm, ask me about, you'll never guess.
3. Each answer ends with a HOOK — a thing a match can naturally comment on or reply to. Otherwise it's a billboard, not a conversation starter.
4. Length: 1–2 sentences per prompt. Hinge users skim.
5. Do not invent details about me that aren't in my input.

OUTPUT IN ORDER:
A. Three answers, one per chosen prompt.
B. One alternative version for each (different angle on the same real detail).
C. A note on which of the three is strongest as a conversation hook.

How to Use This Prompt

  1. Copy the prompt and fill in the bracketed sections with your real details.
  2. Be specific. Vague input produces vague output — that's the whole point of the gates.
  3. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and read the output before using it.
  4. Edit the draft in your own voice before sending or submitting. AI output is the scaffolding, not the final.

Example Output

Prompt: “Two truths and a lie.”

I've never met my dad's side of the family in Wales, I once cooked dinner for a Michelin chef as a 13-year-old, and I cried during the credits of the first John Wick. (The first one is the lie.)

Hinge user replying: ok the Michelin chef thing — how does that even happen.

Tips to Get Better Results

  • Specifics over funny. A weirdly specific detail is more memorable than a clever line.
  • Avoid the “you'll never guess” pattern. Just say the thing.
  • Read each answer as if you're a stranger. If you can't think of a reply, it's not done.
  • Don't recycle the same brag in three different prompts. Three different angles of you.
