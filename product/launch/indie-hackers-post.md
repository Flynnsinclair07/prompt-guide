# Indie Hackers launch post

**Suggested title:**

> Shipped a $39 job-hunt prompt bundle, sold $0 in week 1 — here's what I learned

**Alternative title (slightly less self-deprecating):**

> Week 1 of selling a $39 prompt bundle: traffic, not product, is the bottleneck

**Tagging:** mark it as "Milestone" or share to the "Growth" group. Avoid the "Launch" group — that's reserved for the day-of announcement and you've missed that window.

**Posting time:** Tuesday or Wednesday morning, US Eastern. IH traffic is desktop-heavy and US-skewed.

**Body (~430 words):**

---

I launched The Job Hunter's AI Bundle on May 17 — 44 ChatGPT prompts plus a 118-page workbook for resume, cover letter, LinkedIn, interview, and salary negotiation. $39 on Gumroad, sitting next to a free library of 134 prompt pages at snipprompts.com.

Week 1 results: roughly zero real sales. (Two test purchases I made to verify the delivery flow, refunded immediately.)

I want to be honest about the diagnosis, because every "I shipped something" post on IH leans toward either "here's the playbook that worked" or "here's why the product was wrong." Neither is true here.

The product is not the bottleneck. The free prompt pages have ~unverified-but-growing organic traffic from long-tail searches like "best ChatGPT prompt for resignation letter," and a small number of those visitors hit the Gumroad page. The conversion rate on that page is fine in the abstract — the listing is tight, the pricing rationale lands, the demo video plays. I sent it to four people in my circle who have either bought or refunded; zero of them said "this isn't worth $39." The product passes the most basic offline test.

What I shipped, and what I underestimated, is that a prompt library at a free price point and a structured workflow at a $39 price point look like the same thing to a stranger landing cold. The free site has 134 prompt pages. Why pay for a bundle of 44? The answer is real — the bundle has refuse-to-invent guardrails, a workbook structure, post-output validators, and prompts written for the full job-search arc rather than one slot — but that answer takes 30 seconds of attention to absorb, and 30 seconds is a long time on a sales page.

So week 2 is not "fix the product" and not "rewrite the listing for the 4th time." It's:

1. Run the Product Hunt launch on Wed May 27. Front-page exposure to people who pre-self-select for AI tools.
2. Show HN on Tue or Wed of the following week. HN audience cares about the prompt engineering (guardrails, banned-phrase lists, validators) more than the bundle, but the bundle is in the footer.
3. Reddit, Pinterest, two longform SEO articles on the free site to push the organic-traffic floor higher.

The honest read on week 1 is that I built a product that's good enough, and now I have to do the marketing work that I had been hoping the product would skip for me. Spoiler: products don't do that.

Three things I'd genuinely like feedback on:

1. Is "free library + $39 paid bundle" a clear enough split, or am I cannibalizing? I keep going back and forth.
2. For anyone who's launched on Product Hunt cold (no big mailing list): what was the single highest-leverage thing you did pre-launch?
3. Anyone who buys $30–$50 prompt bundles — what was the line in the listing that flipped you from "interesting" to "buying"?

Thanks for reading. Link in profile if you want to look. Not pasting it here because the post isn't really an ad.

— Flynn
