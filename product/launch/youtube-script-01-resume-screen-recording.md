## 3:25 — 4:50 — The refuse-to-invent gate explained

before we run it, let me say why the refuse-to-invent gate works.

chatgpt's default behavior, especially when you give it a task that requires producing fluent text, is to produce fluent text. it doesn't have a strong default to leave gaps unfilled. if your input has a vague bullet — like "worked on the customer onboarding project at acme" — the model's default is to fill in a metric to make the bullet sound concrete. it picks a plausible number because confident numbers sound better than honest vagueness.

the refuse-to-invent gate flips the model out of that mode. it tells the model, explicitly, that the priority is honesty over fluency. when in doubt, ask. when missing a metric, leave it out and tell the user.

what i've found in practice: on gpt-4 and claude opus, the gate fires about eighty-five to ninety percent of the time. the model genuinely stops and asks. on smaller models — gpt-4o-mini, gpt-3.5, gemini flash — it fires more like sixty or seventy percent. those models lose constraints in long prompts.

we'll see both behaviors live in a minute.

## 6:30 — 8:00 — The response

[Read the response live, react to it.]

okay. look at this. the model didn't write the bullet. it asked me — exactly what we wanted.

[Read the model's question aloud, e.g.:]

"thanks. before i write your bullet, i need a few things. what specific changes did you make to the onboarding flow? do you have any quantifiable outcome — completion rate, time-to-activation, drop-off — that you can share? if not, what was the team's measure of success on this project?"

that is the right behavior. without the gate, the model would have written: "led the redesign of the customer onboarding flow at acme, increasing completion rates by thirty-five percent and reducing time-to-activation by forty percent."

i never said any of that was true. but the model would have invented it anyway because fluent confident bullets read better than honest vague ones.

let me give it a real answer.

[Provide a real-ish answer, e.g.: "I redesigned the email confirmation step and added a single CTA where there used to be four. The team's metric was completion rate. I don't know the exact number but my PM lead told me it went up materially — let's say 'meaningful' — but I can't quote a percentage."]

[Watch the model write the bullet.]

## 8:00 — 9:10 — Critique

okay. here's what the model just wrote:

[Read the actual bullet aloud.]

let me critique it honestly.

what's good: it didn't invent a metric. it described the action — single cta replacing four — and the directional outcome without claiming a specific number. that's defensible in an interview.

what's still a little weak: "meaningful increase" is vague language. if you can get the actual number, get it. dm your old pm lead, ask the analytics team, dig through old slack. real numbers are always stronger than directional ones.

what i'd refine: the bullet currently doesn't say what the project's business impact was, just what i did. for a senior pm bullet, i'd want to add: "shipped on time, owned by me from scope to launch." that signals ownership, which is the senior pm pattern recruiters look for.

so i'd iterate one more round. ask the model to add the ownership framing. and then i'd shop it around — show it to a recruiter friend, get one pair of eyes on it before it goes on the resume.

# WHAT TO DO IF YOU FREEZE WHILE RECORDING

- pause. take a breath. start the sentence over. you can edit out the pause in post.
- if you mess up a section, finish the section, then say "let me redo that" and re-record the section. cut the bad take in iMovie.
- do not try to do this in one perfect take. nobody does. iMovie cuts are the entire point.
- target: 90 minutes total for the first recording session (script-read + redos + breaks). videos 2-3 will go faster.
