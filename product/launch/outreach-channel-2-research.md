# 2nd cold-outreach channel — research (May 2026)

Channel 1 (university career centers) is live and the validated student-builder angle works. This doc scopes the next channel to open *after* the career-center data lands (~June), so we're not spreading thin pre-PH.

The bundle's buyer = someone actively job-hunting. The best channel reaches a roomful of those people through one gatekeeper who already wants to help them. Ranked by fit:

## Option A — Coding bootcamp career-services teams (BEST FIT)
**Why:** bootcamp grads are job-hunting *immediately and intensely* — most land roles in 3–6 months and the school's reputation depends on placement rates (many advertise 70–90%+). Career services is a dedicated team whose entire job is getting grads hired. A free resume/cover-letter/interview resource is directly on-mission for them.
- **Targets:** General Assembly, Springboard, Coding Dojo, Hack Reactor/Galvanize, App Academy, Codesmith, Flatiron, Nucamp, regional/university-affiliated bootcamps (e.g. Stony Brook Career Bootcamps).
- **Gatekeeper:** "Career Services" / "Career Coach" / "Employer Partnerships" — usually a named, findable team (unlike a generic .edu inbox).
- **Angle:** same student-builder honesty, adapted: "I'm a CU Boulder freshman who built a free job-search prompt library — thought your grads in active job search might find it useful. No catch, it's free." Then the bundle as an optional mention, not the pitch.
- **Watch-out:** some bootcamps push their own AI tooling; lead with the free site, not the paid bundle.

## Option B — Public library job-help / adult services
**Why:** libraries run free resume workshops and job-search help desks; librarians actively curate free resource lists for patrons and have no commercial filter — a genuinely free tool is easy for them to share or add to a LibGuide.
- **Targets:** mid-to-large city library systems with a "Careers & Job Search" guide (most have one). Contact = "Adult Services" / "Business & Career Librarian."
- **Angle:** "free, no-signup job-search prompts for your careers resource page." Libraries love linkable free resources. This is low-volume per hit but high trust and good for backlinks (.gov/.org library domains = SEO juice).
- **Watch-out:** slow, bureaucratic; treat as a backlink + goodwill play, not a sales channel.

## Option C — Workforce centers / American Job Centers (CareerOneStop network)
**Why:** state/federal-funded centers serve job seekers directly (unemployment, career transitions). High volume of exactly-our-audience.
- **Targets:** state workforce boards, American Job Centers (one-stops).
- **Angle:** free resource for their job seekers.
- **Watch-out:** the most bureaucratic of the three; public-sector procurement instincts make them wary of anything that looks like a vendor. Lowest priority — only if A and B underperform.

## Recommendation
1. **Start with A (bootcamps)** once career-center data is in (~June). Same playbook, same proven angle, warmer fit than universities.
2. **Layer in B (libraries)** as a parallel backlink/goodwill track — different ask (link on a resource page), so it doesn't compete for the same reply.
3. **Hold C** unless needed.

## Reuse
- Same tracker format as `career-centers-tracker.md`.
- Same student-builder opener (see `feedback_student_builder_angle` in memory) — it's the thing that kills the AI/vendor tone.
- Build a batch-1 target list (10–15 bootcamps) before sending; verify the career-services contact name, don't format-guess emails (SDSU/Cal Poly bounces were format-guesses).
