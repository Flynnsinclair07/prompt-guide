# Homepage above-the-fold stranger-test — review

I read `index.html` as a first-time PH visitor at 1:30 AM on Wednesday. Here's the actual punch list.

## What a stranger sees in the first 3 seconds (in order)

1. **Bundle banner** (top strip): "The Job Hunter's AI Bundle — 44 ChatGPT prompts, 8 negotiation scripts, and 3 worksheets for the full job search. Get the bundle →"
2. **Hero H1**: "The Best AI Prompt for Anything"
3. **Subhero**: "Free, tested prompts for ChatGPT & Claude. Copy, paste, get results."
4. **Tagline**: "Made by one person in Colorado. Every prompt tested before it shipped."
5. **Search bar**: "Search 170+ prompts..."
6. **Story (paragraph 1)**: "I built this because every ChatGPT resume prompt I tried came back claiming I'd 'spearheaded cross-functional synergy'..."

## What works

- **Bundle banner is the right move.** PH visitors who came for the paid product see it immediately. Single-click conversion.
- **"Made by one person in Colorado" tagline.** The single most distinguishing line on the page — competitors can't copy a person. Keeps E-E-A-T strong.
- **"Spearheaded cross-functional synergy" specific example.** Unfakeable, memorable, signals you understand the problem.
- **Search bar puts the user in control.** Better than scrolling through hundreds of cards.

## What's still weak (positioning calls — your decision)

These I won't change unilaterally because they're positioning decisions, not typos:

### 1. The "for Anything" positioning vs the visible job-hunt focus

The hero says "The Best AI Prompt for Anything." But the bundle banner above and the first 10 cards below are ALL job-hunt-specific (cover letter, resume, LinkedIn, interview, thank-you, follow-up, salary, two-weeks-notice...).

A PH visitor lands and has to spend 1-2 seconds figuring out "is this generic prompts or specifically job hunting?" That ambiguity costs conversions during a high-traffic launch when attention is the scarcest resource.

**Two options:**
- **Option A (positioning shift):** change H1 to "The Best ChatGPT Prompts for the Job Hunt" or "AI Prompts for the Whole Job Search." Sharper, matches the bundle, matches the cards, makes the offer instantly clear to PH visitors. Tradeoff: gives up the "for anything" generalist framing. For PH-launch-day specifically, sharper > broader.
- **Option B (leave for now):** keep the generalist framing. PH visitors who scroll figure it out in 2 seconds. The site DOES have non-job-hunt prompts (recipes, meal planning, fitness, etc.), and narrowing the H1 might hurt long-tail SEO for those.

**My read:** for PH launch day specifically, Option A converts better. For evergreen SEO, Option B is broader. The compromise is a SUBTITLE under the H1: keep "The Best AI Prompt for Anything" but add "Especially the job search — resume, cover letter, interview, salary negotiation, the whole arc." That gets both audiences in one line.

If you want me to make that change, say "go option A," "go subtitle compromise," or "leave it."

### 2. The bundle banner is the brightest thing on the page

The banner is the strongest visual + first thing in the eye scan. That's deliberate (you want PH visitors who came for the paid product). But it also signals "we want your $39" before "here's what's free." For the visitors who haven't decided yet, that ordering may push them away.

**The trade-off:** a quieter banner (smaller, less visually loud) softens the sales feel but also lowers click-through from PH visitors who came specifically for the bundle.

**My read:** leave it for launch day. Soften it post-PH if Thursday's data shows the bundle banner is converting < 1% of homepage visitors.

### 3. Story paragraph 1 is great. Story paragraph 2 is weaker.

Paragraph 1 ("I built this because every ChatGPT resume prompt...") is the strongest piece of marketing copy on the entire site. Specific, unfakeable, vulnerable, clear.

Paragraph 2 ("The prompts here refuse to invent — they ask for your real numbers...") is good but more abstract. It lists three features. A first-time visitor doesn't yet have the context to value "refuse-to-invent gates" or "banned-phrase lists." Those land for a return visitor; not for a stranger.

**Possible rewrite of paragraph 2** (only if you want — feels like a minor thing):
> "So I started writing prompts that refused. Refused to invent metrics I didn't give them. Refused the corporate clichés. Refused the median version. 170+ of them now, all free."

Shorter, more concrete, lands faster.
