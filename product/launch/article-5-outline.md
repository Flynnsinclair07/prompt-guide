## Section: Why ChatGPT sounds like AI (the 4 root causes)
The model defaults you're fighting:
1. **It fills gaps with plausible filler** — invents specifics rather than asking for them.
2. **It reaches for the statistical median** — the most common phrasing, which is cliché by definition ("results-driven," "in today's fast-paced world").
3. **It over-structures** — perfectly balanced paragraphs, even sentence lengths, visible scaffolding ("Firstly... Secondly... In conclusion").
4. **It hedges and pads** — "it's important to note," "in order to," "that being said" — filler that no human under deadline writes.

## Section: The 6 principles that fix it (the core of the article)
1. **Feed it specifics; never let it invent.** Add the refuse-to-invent rule. (link to resume article)
2. **Ban the tells.** Keep a banned-phrase list in your prompt. (the cliché list)
3. **Constrain the structure.** Tell it to vary sentence length, drop the "firstly/secondly" scaffolding, no summary paragraph.
4. **Cut the filler verbs and hedges.** "in order to" → "to"; delete "it's important to note."
5. **Read it out loud.** The single best AI-detector you own — if you wouldn't say it, cut it. (link to interview article)
6. **Edit the open and close yourself.** The first and last lines are where AI is most obvious and where your voice matters most.

Honest nuance — for low-stakes stuff, AI-polish is fine. For anything where a human is judging YOU (application, outreach, public writing), the AI-tell costs you. Match effort to stakes.
