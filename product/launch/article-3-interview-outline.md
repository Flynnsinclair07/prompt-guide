# Article #3 — Outline

## Hook (3 paragraphs, ~250 words)

**Para 1:** Open with the specific failure mode. Reader pasted "tell me about a time you led a team" into ChatGPT, got back a 4-paragraph answer following perfect STAR structure, memorized it, walked into the interview, said it word-for-word, interviewer's eyes glazed over by sentence two. The answer was correct. It also sounded like it was being read off a teleprompter.

**Para 2:** Why ChatGPT interview answers fail differently than resumes and cover letters. With resumes the model invents facts. With cover letters it defaults to clichés. With **interview answers, the failure is delivery, not content** — the model writes a textbook-perfect answer that's impossible to deliver naturally. The structure is so visible the listener can hear the seams.

**Para 3:** The fix isn't to skip ChatGPT — it's to use it differently for interviews than for written deliverables. ChatGPT can be your sparring partner; it can't be your script.

## Section: Why ChatGPT interview answers sound rehearsed (the mechanic)

Two reasons:

1. **The corpus is full of "how to answer the X interview question" articles** — most of which are written *prescriptively* (here's THE answer to use). The model has read 100,000 versions of "the correct STAR answer for tell-me-about-a-conflict" and produces a confident average of them.

2. **Spoken language is fundamentally different from written language**, and ChatGPT only generates written language. Real spoken answers have false starts, mid-sentence corrections, varying sentence lengths, casual transitions ("yeah so like..." / "I guess what I'm saying is..."). ChatGPT generates clean prose because that's what it's trained on. Clean prose in your mouth sounds rehearsed.

## Section: The four failure modes to watch for

1. **Perfect STAR structure delivered word-for-word.** Situation → Task → Action → Result is fine as a scaffolding. It's not fine as a script. Real people don't say "The situation was..."
2. **Vocabulary that doesn't sound like you.** ChatGPT will give you "stakeholder alignment" and "cross-functional coordination" — words you'd never use in a 1:1 with your boss.
3. **Even pacing and length.** Every answer comes back 90-120 seconds. Real conversation has 30-second answers and 3-minute answers depending on the question.
4. **Generic enthusiasm in the outcome.** "...and I learned a lot about leadership and effective communication." (Filler that signals: I am wrapping up the answer now because I read in a guide that I should end on a learning.)

## Section: The right process, step by step

### Step 1: Use ChatGPT to find your stories, not write them.

The most useful prompt isn't "write me a STAR answer for X." It's "given my background, what 5-7 stories from my last 2 jobs would best demonstrate [leadership / ambiguous projects / conflict / mistakes / cross-functional work / tight deadlines]?" Have ChatGPT mine your own input for material.

### Step 2: Draft each story in bullet points, not paragraphs.

You're not writing prose. You're writing a story skeleton. 5-7 bullets per story: Situation, the main constraint, what you decided to do and why, what you actually did, the outcome (with numbers if you have them), what you'd do differently.

### Step 3: Talk through the bullets out loud. Don't memorize sentences.

This is the step that breaks the rehearsed feel. Read the bullets, then close the doc and tell the story to your phone's voice memo as if you were telling a friend. The exact words change every time you tell it. That's the goal — variability is what natural sounds like.

### Step 4: Run the AI-grilling drill.

Use ChatGPT as the toughest version of your interviewer. Paste your story, ask: "follow up on this answer the way the toughest version of this hiring manager would — push on the specifics, the decisions, the numbers, the contributions of other team members." This is where memorized answers die — interviewers always ask follow-ups. If your answer was a script, you're now lost.

### Step 5: Cut the meta-language.

"In this situation..." "The key takeaway was..." "What I learned from this experience..." All filler signals you're delivering a prepared answer. Real people just tell the story. Remove the scaffolding language; let the story stand.

### Step 6: Record yourself one last time.

Talk through 3-4 of your prepared stories on your phone's voice memo, then listen. If a sentence makes you cringe to hear yourself say, cut it. Trust the cringe — that's the AI tell your interviewer would catch.

## Section: When to stop using ChatGPT for interview prep

1. **The "why this company" question.** Has to come from your real research. If ChatGPT generates it, it sounds like ChatGPT generated it.
2. **Salary negotiation talking points.** Different problem, different tool (link to negotiation prompt or bundle's Module 5).
3. **Questions you ask the interviewer.** Generated questions sound generated. Yours need to come from real curiosity about the role.
4. **The "tell me about yourself" answer.** This one's so high-stakes and so personal that you should write it yourself, then have ChatGPT tighten it — never the other way around.

## Section: The short version

ChatGPT interview answers sound rehearsed because the training data is written prose, and written prose in your mouth always sounds rehearsed. The fix: use ChatGPT to mine your stories and grill you on follow-ups, but speak the answers in your own words every time. Variability is the signal that you're a real person who lived these experiences, not someone who memorized a script.
