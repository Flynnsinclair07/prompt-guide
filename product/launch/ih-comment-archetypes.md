# Indie Hackers comment archetypes — for unlocking the `flynn` account

Daily, 2–3 min: open indiehackers.com, scan the homepage feed. Comment on ONE post that fits an archetype below. The point is to be the comment a founder remembers — specific, useful, brief.

**Why these unlock the account:** IH's anti-spam ranking weights comment-thread engagement and quality. ~10–20 thoughtful comments over a couple of weeks gets you to "post-eligible." Then your milestone post (`indie-hackers-post.md`) lands without hitting the moderation queue.

## Anti-rules across all archetypes

- Never link your own site in the first 20 comments. After your account is established, link only when directly relevant AND in a reply, not a top-level comment.
- Be specific, brief, and answer the question the OP actually asked. IH skews technical and founder-savvy; vague support comments get downvoted.
- Don't post "great post!" or "love this!" — counts as spam.
- One comment per day until the account unlocks. Bursts look like a campaign.
- Match length to the post. A 300-word OP gets a 60-word comment, not a 300-word essay.

## Archetype 1: "I just shipped X — feedback?"

The OP posted a launch announcement, link to their site, and is asking for input.

**Comment pattern:**

> [Specific compliment on one concrete thing — the niche, the positioning, the pricing model, the landing-page copy, a specific feature.]
> 
> One thing I'd push on: [specific actionable critique tied to that compliment]. [One sentence on WHY.]
> 
> [Optional: one question that helps them think about something they probably haven't been asked yet.]

**Example for a fictional "I built a tool for X" post:**
> The pricing tier breakdown reads cleaner than most early SaaS pages I see — no "contact us" tier, no fake "most popular" badge, just three numbers. Worth keeping.
> 
> One thing I'd push on: the headline says "the easiest way to do X" but the demo video doesn't show a comparison to the existing-default way. Easiest-than-what is doing all the work in that sentence and you're leaving it implicit.
> 
> Curious which two competitors you keep losing comparison conversations to — that's usually where the headline rewrite lives.

**Customize:** actually click their link before commenting. The credibility of the whole comment dies if you didn't.

## Archetype 2: "How did you find your first 10 customers?"

**Comment pattern:**

> Different answer for different products, but in my (small) sample the pattern that's worked: [one specific tactic with one specific number].
> 
> [Brief explanation of why that tactic worked in that case.]
> 
> What hasn't worked: [the counterintuitive thing — usually the channel everyone tries first].

**Example:**
> Different answer per product, but in my (small) sample the pattern that's worked: cold-emailing the most-aligned, smallest possible target list — for a job-search tool that's individual university career-services directors, not students. 21 personalized emails got 3 real conversations and 1 active referrer. Replied rate ~10%, much higher than any social channel.
> 
> What hasn't worked at this stage: paid ads. The CAC for an early product is unknowable, and the data is too noisy at low volume to optimize against.

**Customize:** if you've actually done a tactic the OP is asking about, share the specific number and the surprise.

## Archetype 3: "I just hit [milestone] — AMA"

The OP posted a milestone (first $1K, first 100 users, hit ramen profitability). They want engagement, not advice.

**Comment pattern:**

> Congrats. One specific question: [thing only someone who's also building would think to ask]. [Optional: brief context for why you're asking.]

**Examples:**
> Congrats. One question: when did the conversion rate on the homepage stabilize, and what was driving the noise before that? I keep losing days to homepage-tweaking and I'm not sure any of it is real signal vs. just my visitor mix changing.

> Congrats. What was the single biggest false start in your first 6 months — the thing you spent a lot of time on that turned out to be the wrong call?

**Customize:** ask something they specifically would have insight on. Generic "what was your biggest lesson" gets ignored.

## Archetype 4: "Should I [pricing / positioning / pivot] decision"

The OP is asking the community for input on a structural decision.

**Comment pattern:**

> The thing I'd anchor on isn't [the surface question] — it's [the deeper question the surface question is a proxy for]. Reason: [one-line why].
> 
> [Specific suggestion or framing that addresses the deeper question.]

**Example for a "should I lower my price?" post:**
> The thing I'd anchor on isn't whether $49 vs. $29 is "right" — it's what your current refund rate is and what conversations you're having with people who didn't buy. Reason: a price drop solves a conversion problem; if your refund rate is already low and the people who DO buy aren't churning, the issue is reach, not price.
> 
> Concrete: split-test the SAME price with a different headline before you split-test the price.

**Customize:** when you can re-frame their question into a better question, that's the comment that gets pinned.

## Archetype 5: "What tool / stack / process should I use for [X]"

**Comment pattern:**

> Quick answer + the trade-off:
> 
> [The thing you'd recommend], because [the one reason it wins for the OP's stated context].
> 
> The trade-off: [the thing that makes other options sometimes better]. If [condition], pick [alternative] instead.

**Example for a "what should I use for email?" post:**
> Quick answer + trade-off:
> 
> Kit (formerly ConvertKit) on the free tier wins for a content-led product like yours, because the form embed is friction-free and the deliverability is better than the cheap alternatives.
> 
> Trade-off: free tier caps you at 1 sequence and limited automation logic. If you have a multi-product email funnel from day 1, that's a real ceiling — start on Beehiiv or even MailerLite. For a one-product, one-list operation, Kit's free tier is the right starting point.

**Customize:** answer as someone who has actually used the tool. Generic "use Stripe" comments without the trade-off are everywhere and ignored.

## Tracking

For each comment:
- Date + post URL
- Your comment's net upvotes after 24 hours
- Whether OP replied
- Whether anyone in the thread asked a follow-up

You're warmed once you have ~10 comments that got OP-replies. At that point post the milestone announcement (`indie-hackers-post.md`).
