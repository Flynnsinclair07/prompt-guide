# Competitor research — prompt sites & positioning (May 2026)

Quick scan of the prompt-library landscape and where SnipPrompts actually fits. The point isn't to copy them — it's to find the gap we already sit in and lean harder into it.

## The big players

### 1. FlowGPT
- Community-driven, huge volume, "trending" feed of user-submitted prompts. Free.
- **Strength:** scale and recency — thousands of prompts, social signals.
- **Weakness:** quality is a lottery. No editorial standard, no testing, no voice. You wade through junk to find one good prompt.
- **What we take:** nothing structural. Their weakness is our whole pitch.

### 2. Awesome ChatGPT Prompts / prompts.chat (open source, GitHub)
- The largest open-source list. Cross-model. Developer audience.
- **Strength:** trust (open source), breadth, SEO authority.
- **Weakness:** it's a flat list of one-liners ("Act as a..."), no use-case depth, no examples, no human behind it.
- **What we take:** their one-liners are shallow. Our pages (full prompt + how-to + example + tips + books) are 10x deeper per topic — that's the SEO moat.

### 3. God of Prompt / PromptHero / AIPRM (paid-leaning)
- God of Prompt: free to browse, email-gated mega-prompts, sells bundles. PromptHero: image-heavy. AIPRM: Chrome extension injecting prompts into ChatGPT.
- **Strength:** monetization is figured out (bundles, pro tiers, extension).
- **Weakness:** generic, corporate, no point of view. "30,000+ prompts" is a quantity flex, not a quality one.
- **What we take:** the bundle model works (validates our $39 bundle). But their volume-brag positioning is the opposite of ours.

## Where SnipPrompts is different (and should stay)
1. **A real person, one point of view.** "Made by one person in Colorado, tested before it shipped." None of the big sites have a face or a voice. That's our edge with humans and, increasingly, with Google (E-E-A-T).
2. **Anti-template as the thesis.** Every competitor sells "more prompts." We sell "prompts that refuse to sound like AI." The 4 long-form guides are the proof. No one else is publishing the *failure modes*.
3. **Depth per page, not count.** One deep page per intent (prompt + example + how-to + FAQ + books) beats a 30k flat list for long-tail SEO. We rank for "chatgpt prompt for a two weeks notice letter," they rank for nothing specific.
4. **Job-search niche + a real product behind it.** The bundle gives the free content a destination the pure libraries don't have.

## Positioning ideas worth stealing/testing
- **Lean into the face.** Add a small author byline + photo on articles (E-E-A-T; competitors can't copy a person). Already have "Made by one person in Colorado" — push it.
- **"Tested" as a badge.** Competitors can't claim every prompt was tested. Consider a tiny "tested" marker on pages — only if it's true.
- **Comparison content (long game):** a future article like "FlowGPT vs a curated prompt — why more prompts isn't better" captures competitor-brand search. Don't do pre-PH; note for the SEO pipeline.

## Don't copy
- The "30,000+ prompts" quantity race — it's a trust-killer and not our game.
- Email-gating the actual prompts (God of Prompt does this). Our free prompts stay free and ungated; the email capture is a soft offer, not a wall.
