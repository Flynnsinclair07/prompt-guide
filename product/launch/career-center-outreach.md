# College career center outreach — email template

**Use case:** cold email to career services directors at colleges and universities. Goal is to offer the free prompt library as a student resource — no bundle mention, no commercial ask. The win is link inclusion in their student-facing resources page or career newsletter.

**Targeting:** career services directors at mid-size U.S. universities (5k–25k undergrads) are the sweet spot — small enough that the director reads cold email, large enough that one link inclusion gets meaningful traffic. Find the director's name on the career center's "Staff" page before sending; "[Career Center Director]" is a placeholder of last resort.

**Subject line options (test in batches of ~20):**

- A: A free ChatGPT prompt library for your students
- B: 134 free ChatGPT prompts your students can use for resumes and interviews
- C: Resource for your students — free, no signup

Default to A. B is slightly more specific. C is the shortest, most action-oriented and may perform best in busy inboxes.

**Email body (151 words):**

---

hey,

I run snipprompts.com — a free library of 134 ChatGPT prompts covering resumes, cover letters, LinkedIn profiles, interview prep, and salary negotiation. No signup, no paywall, no email capture required to use any of them.

Students are using ChatGPT for job-search work whether career centers endorse it or not. The risk is they're using one-line prompts that produce generic output, or worse, prompts that quietly invent experience they don't have. The free pages on snipprompts.com are structured to prevent that — every prompt has guardrails that tell the model to ask for missing information rather than fabricate it, plus a banned-phrase list to keep the output from sounding like AI.

If it'd be useful for your students, you're welcome to link to it from your resources page or newsletter. Happy to answer any questions about how the prompts are built.

— Flynn
snipprompts.com

---

**Notes:**
- Opens with lowercase "hey,". Body is sentence case. Proper nouns properly cased (ChatGPT, LinkedIn).
- One link, in the signature. Listed plainly, not as a CTA button.
- No bundle mention anywhere. The bundle is invisible to the recipient — they see the free library only. Career center directors will not link to commercial products as student resources; they will link to free ones. The angle has to be 100% free.
- The "AI is already happening, here's the safer version" framing is the actual hook. Career center directors are conflicted about ChatGPT; positioning the site as the responsible-use option converts better than positioning it as a general resource.
- "Happy to answer questions" is a low-friction reply CTA without being a sales pitch.
- Sign-off is `— Flynn` then `snipprompts.com` on its own line, matching the canonical receipt-email pattern from brand-voice.md.

**Operational notes (for Flynn, not in the email):**
- Personalize the first line with one specific detail from the school's career page when you can ("I noticed [School] runs a resume review program every Thursday"). Cold sends with zero personalization land at 1–3%; one personalized detail moves it to 8–12%.
- Send Tuesday or Wednesday morning, 9–11 AM in the recipient's time zone. Avoid Friday afternoons and Monday mornings.
- Don't bulk-send. 20 per batch, with a manual review of each personalization, is realistic. 100+ sends without personalization will land in spam.
- Track replies. The pattern that converts is "this is useful, can you tell me more / send a one-pager" — have a one-page PDF ready that summarizes the library and the anti-fabrication angle.
- Don't follow up more than once. If they don't reply to the first email plus one 7-day-later nudge, they aren't interested.
